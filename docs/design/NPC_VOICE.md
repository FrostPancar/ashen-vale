# ASHEN VALE — NPC Voice Guide

Source of truth for all NPC dialog, sign text, quest titles, and flavor copy.
If new text doesn't pass this guide, rewrite it before shipping.

---

## 1. The voice in one sentence

**Weary, dry-witted villagers at the edge of something old and wrong — who would still
rather make a joke than a speech.**

The vale is dying slowly and everyone knows it. Nobody wails about it. They farm, they
forge, they over-charge for tonics, and they hand the problem to the first stranger the
bell wakes up. The humor is a coping mechanism, and that's what makes the warmth real.

## 2. Inspirations

| Source | What we take | What we leave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pokémon Gen 1 NPCs** | Brevity. One idea per text box. Strangers who tell you odd, useful things unprompted. | Childishness, fourth-wall cheer |
| **Zelda: Link's Awakening** | Quirky townsfolk with one strong gimmick each; melancholy under the whimsy | Surreal randomness |
| **Dark Souls / Diablo** | Understated doom. Big threats described in small, tired sentences. Item-text gravity | Opaque lore-dumping, despair |
| **Terry Pratchett** | Dry institutional humor ("it makes the paperwork easier"), bathos, kind cynicism | Long riffs — we have ~140 characters |

The mix is roughly: **Pokémon's economy + Souls' dread + Pratchett's delivery.**

## 3. Tone pillars

1. **Dread, undercut.** State the horror plainly, then puncture it with something small
   and human. Never puncture the horror so hard it stops being scary.
   > "Slay the STONE WARDEN... Come back alive — it makes the paperwork easier."
2. **Gruff outside, warm inside.** Nobody says "I care about you." They say
   "Maren covers heroes' tabs." Affection is shown through actions and grumbling.
3. **Everyone has a job.** NPCs talk about their own lives — the forge, the inn, the
   road toll — not about being NPCs in an adventure. The hero is a disruption to their day.
4. **The world is old.** Reference things that happened long before the player: wards,
   rusted levers, buried road-toll loot, "a Warden of old stone, gone wrong."
5. **Hopeful, barely.** The vale is fading, not doomed. The last line of any arc should
   leave a light on ("The bell rings lighter since you came.").

## 4. Language rules

### Format (hard limits)
- **One idea per page.** Max ~140 characters / 2–3 rendered lines per dialog page.
- **1–3 pages** for plot NPCs, **1 page** for flavor NPCs and signs.
- **ALL CAPS** for proper nouns the player must remember (ELDER MAREN, STONE WARDEN,
  ASHFALL) and for key prompts in tutorial text (LEFT CLICK, E).
- Em dashes and ellipses are the house punctuation: `—` for a turn of thought,
  `...` for trailing dread. Use sparingly — once per page at most.
- Stage directions in asterisks only for sound: `*hic*`. No `*smiles*`-style emotes.

### Diction
- **Concrete, rural, pre-industrial.** Hearth, steel, straw, wards, tonics, carts,
  paperwork (the one bureaucratic joke we allow — it's load-bearing).
- **Short words, short sentences.** Fragments are encouraged: "Good steel. Good arm."
- Contractions yes. Modern slang never ("okay", "guys", "awesome" are banned).
- No archaic cosplay either — "thee/thou/'tis" is banned. These are working people,
  not renaissance faire actors.
- Directions are given by **landmark + compass**: "south-east, past the fountain."
  Never by game coordinates or menus (except Wick — see below).

### Tutorial text
Mechanics hints must be **diegetic or framed as a joke about being diegetic**:
> WICK: "Press I for your pack, K for your craft. An old family saying."

Signs may be bluntly instructional but should still carry tone:
> "TRAINING YARD — strike the dummies. LEFT CLICK to attack."

### Signs
Signs are NPCs without bodies. Same wit, more compressed:
> "Hollow Cave. The dark eats lanterns. It will not eat you. Probably."
> "ASHFALL — second city of the vale. Population: anxious."

### Quest titles
2–4 words, evocative, ALL CAPS, no colons: `THE BELL STILL RINGS`, `STEEL FOR THE ROAD`,
`PROVE YOUR ARM`. Objectives are imperative and landmark-anchored.

## 5. Naming conventions

Short, rustic, one-or-two syllables, faintly English-village: **Maren, Bram, Posy,
Het, Toll, Fern, Wick, Pip, Drew.** Epithets over surnames ("OLD HET", "SOGGY DREW").
Monsters and places get heavy compound names in caps: STONE WARDEN, HOLLOW ROAD, ASHFALL.

If two NPCs could swap lines without anyone noticing, one of them needs a rewrite.

## 7. Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| "The dark eats lanterns. It will not eat you. Probably." | "Beware, brave traveler, for untold horrors lurk within the stygian depths!" |
| "Forge is hot, patience is thin." | "Greetings! I am Bram, the town blacksmith. How may I assist you today?" |
| "Road's open for you, hero. Slimes by the bridge... Walk loud." | "Quest accepted! Head north and defeat 5 slimes." |
| "The bell rings lighter since you came." | "Thank you for saving our village! You are our hero!" |

## 8. Checklist for new dialog

- [ ] Each page ≤ ~140 chars, one idea
- [ ] Proper nouns the player must remember are ALL CAPS
- [ ] Directions use landmark + compass
- [ ] At least one line does double duty (lore + hint, or joke + warning)
- [ ] The dread is real and the joke doesn't kill it
- [ ] The NPC sounds like nobody else in the cast
- [ ] No modern slang, no fantasy-archaic, no exclamation-point cheer (Pip excepted)
- [ ] Final stage of any arc leaves a light on
